4. How much heat is needed to raise 2 kilograms of water by 10 degrees Celsius? Take the specific heat of water as 4,200 joules per kilogram per kelvin.
   - Options: **84,000 joules** / 42,000 joules / 8,400 joules / 840,000 joules
   - Explanation: Heat is m c times the temperature change: 2 times 4,200 times 10 is 84,000 joules.
5. Heat flows from one body to another because of a difference in their:
   - Options: **temperature** / mass / heat capacity / volume
   - Explanation: Heat always flows from the hotter body to the colder one.

## Class 10 Chemistry

### Periodic Properties and Variations

1. Across a period from left to right, atomic size generally:
   - Options: **decreases** / increases / stays the same / first increases and then decreases
   - Explanation: Nuclear charge increases across a period and pulls the electrons closer.
2. Down a group, metallic character:
   - Options: **increases** / decreases / stays the same / disappears
   - Explanation: Atoms get larger down a group and lose electrons more easily.
3. Which is the most electronegative element?
   - Options: **fluorine** / oxygen / chlorine / nitrogen
   - Explanation: Fluorine is small with a high nuclear charge, so it attracts shared electrons most strongly.
4. The energy needed to remove an electron from an isolated gaseous atom is called its:
   - Options: **ionisation potential** / electron affinity / electronegativity / atomic radius
   - Explanation: Ionisation potential, or ionisation energy, is the energy to remove the outermost electron.
5. Down a group, ionisation potential:
   - Options: **decreases** / increases / stays the same / first decreases and then increases
   - Explanation: The outer electrons are farther from the nucleus and easier to remove.

### Acids, Bases and Salts

1. A solution with a p H of 3 is:
   - Options: **acidic** / alkaline / neutral / pure water
   - Explanation: A p H below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, and above 7 is alkaline.
2. An acid reacts with a base to form:
   - Options: **a salt and water** / a salt and hydrogen / only water / oxygen and water
   - Explanation: This is neutralisation.
3. Phenolphthalein turns pink in:
   - Options: **an alkaline solution** / an acidic solution / pure water / a neutral salt solution
   - Explanation: Phenolphthalein is colourless in acidic and neutral solutions and pink in alkalis.
4. Which of these is an alkali?
   - Options: **sodium hydroxide** / copper oxide / hydrochloric acid / sodium chloride
   - Explanation: An alkali is a base that dissolves in water; copper oxide is a base but insoluble.
5. What is the p H of pure water at room temperature?
   - Options: **7** / 0 / 14 / 1
   - Explanation: Pure water is neutral, and neutral means p H 7.

### Mole Concept and Stoichiometry

1. Avogadro's number is approximately:
   - Options: **6.022 times 10 to the power 23** / 6.022 times 10 to the power 22 / 3.011 times 10 to the power 23 / 22.4
   - Explanation: One mole of any substance contains 6.022 times 10 to the power 23 particles.
2. What volume does one mole of any gas occupy at S T P?
   - Options: **22.4 litres** / 11.2 litres / 1 litre / 6.022 litres
   - Explanation: The molar volume of a gas at S T P is 22.4 litres.
3. How many moles are there in 36 grams of water?
   - Options: **2** / 1 / 3 / 36
   - Explanation: The molar mass of water is 18 grams, and 36 divided by 18 is 2.
4. What is the molar mass of carbon dioxide? Take carbon as 12 and oxygen as 16.
   - Options: **44 grams** / 28 grams / 32 grams / 60 grams
   - Explanation: C O 2 is 12 plus 2 times 16, which is 44.
5. How many moles of oxygen molecules are there in 64 grams of oxygen gas? Take oxygen as 16.
   - Options: **2** / 4 / 1 / 64
   - Explanation: An oxygen molecule, O 2, has a molar mass of 32 grams, and 64 divided by 32 is 2.

### Electrolysis

1. During electrolysis, positive ions, or cations, move towards the:
   - Options: **cathode** / anode / both electrodes equally / neither electrode
   - Explanation: The cathode is the negative electrode, so it attracts positive ions.
2. Which of these is a strong electrolyte?
   - Options: **sodium chloride solution** / acetic acid / carbon tetrachloride / sugar solution
   - Explanation: Sodium chloride ionises completely; acetic acid is weak; the other two are non-electrolytes.
3. When an article is electroplated with silver, the article is made the:
   - Options: **cathode** / anode / electrolyte / salt bridge
   - Explanation: Silver ions are reduced and deposit on the cathode; a silver block is the anode.
4. During electrolysis, oxidation takes place at the:
   - Options: **anode** / cathode / electrolyte / power supply
   - Explanation: Negative ions lose electrons at the anode, which is oxidation.
5. During the electrolysis of acidified water, which ions are discharged at the cathode?
   - Options: **hydrogen ions** / hydroxide ions / sulphate ions / chloride ions
   - Explanation: Positive hydrogen ions gain electrons at the cathode and form hydrogen gas.

### Organic Chemistry

1. What is the general formula of the alkanes?
   - Options: **C n H 2 n plus 2** / C n H 2 n / C n H 2 n minus 2 / C n H n
   - Explanation: Alkanes are saturated; C n H 2 n is alkenes and C n H 2 n minus 2 is alkynes.
2. Consecutive members of a homologous series differ by:
   - Options: **a C H 2 group** / a C H 3 group / an O H group / two carbon atoms
   - Explanation: Each member has one more C H 2 unit, which is 14 mass units, than the one before.
3. What is the first member of the alkene series?
   - Options: **ethene** / methane / ethane / ethyne
   - Explanation: An alkene needs a carbon-carbon double bond, so the smallest has two carbons: ethene.
4. Which of these is an unsaturated hydrocarbon?
   - Options: **ethyne** / methane / ethane / propane
   - Explanation: Ethyne has a triple bond; the others are alkanes with only single bonds.
5. What is the I U P A C name of the alkane with two carbon atoms?
   - Options: **ethane** / methane / ethene / propane
   - Explanation: The prefix eth means two carbons and the ending ane means an alkane.

## Class 10 Biology

### Genetics

1. Who is known as the father of genetics?
   - Options: **Gregor Mendel** / Charles Darwin / Hugo de Vries / Robert Hooke
   - Explanation: Mendel worked out the laws of inheritance using pea plants.
2. How many chromosomes does a normal human body cell have?
   - Options: **46** / 23 / 44 / 48
   - Explanation: Body cells have 23 pairs, which is 46; gametes have 23.
3. Two heterozygous tall pea plants, T t, are crossed. What is the ratio of tall to dwarf offspring?
   - Options: **3 to 1** / 1 to 1 / 2 to 1 / all tall
   - Explanation: The offspring are 1 T T, 2 T t, and 1 t t, so 3 tall to 1 dwarf.
4. The sex of a child in humans is decided by the chromosome received from the:
   - Options: **father** / mother / both parents equally / neither parent
   - Explanation: The mother always gives an X; the father gives an X, for a girl, or a Y, for a boy.
5. An allele that shows its effect even when only one copy is present is called:
   - Options: **dominant** / recessive / mutant / sex-linked
   - Explanation: A dominant allele masks the recessive one in a heterozygous individual.

### Photosynthesis

1. Which gas is released during photosynthesis?
   - Options: **oxygen** / carbon dioxide / nitrogen / hydrogen
   - Explanation: Plants take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen during photosynthesis.
2. The green pigment that traps light energy is:
   - Options: **chlorophyll** / haemoglobin / carotene / xanthophyll
   - Explanation: Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs light; haemoglobin is in blood.
3. The oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from:
   - Options: **water** / carbon dioxide / glucose / chlorophyll
   - Explanation: Water molecules are split in the light reaction, which is photolysis, releasing oxygen.
4. Photosynthesis in a green plant mainly takes place in the:
   - Options: **chloroplasts of leaf cells** / mitochondria of root cells / nucleus of stem cells / xylem vessels
   - Explanation: Chloroplasts, mostly in the mesophyll cells of leaves, contain the chlorophyll.
5. Which of these is a raw material for photosynthesis?
   - Options: **carbon dioxide** / oxygen / glucose / starch
   - Explanation: Plants use carbon dioxide and water; glucose and oxygen are the products.

### Circulatory System

1. Which blood vessels carry blood away from the heart?
   - Options: **arteries** / veins / capillaries / lymph vessels
   - Explanation: Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins bring it back.
2. What is the largest artery in the human body?
   - Options: **the aorta** / the pulmonary vein / the vena cava / the carotid artery
   - Explanation: The aorta carries oxygenated blood from the left ventricle to the body.
3. Which blood cells help in clotting?
   - Options: **platelets** / red blood cells / white blood cells / plasma cells
   - Explanation: Platelets, or thrombocytes, start the clotting process at a wound.
4. The pulmonary vein carries:
   - Options: **oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart** / deoxygenated blood to the lungs / oxygenated blood to the body / deoxygenated blood from the body
   - Explanation: It is the only vein that carries oxygenated blood.
5. How many chambers does the human heart have?
   - Options: **4** / 2 / 3 / 5
   - Explanation: The human heart has two atria and two ventricles.

### Nervous System and Sense Organs

1. What is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system?
   - Options: **the neuron** / the nephron / the alveolus / the axon
   - Explanation: A neuron is a nerve cell; the axon is only one part of it, and the nephron is in the kidney.
2. Which part of the brain controls balance and posture?
   - Options: **cerebellum** / cerebrum / medulla oblongata / hypothalamus
   - Explanation: The cerebellum coordinates muscles and maintains balance.
3. A simple reflex action, like pulling your hand away from a hot object, is controlled by the:
   - Options: **spinal cord** / cerebrum / cerebellum / heart
   - Explanation: The reflex arc passes through the spinal cord, so the response is very fast.
4. On which part of the eye is the image formed?
   - Options: **the retina** / the cornea / the iris / the lens
   - Explanation: The lens focuses light on the retina, which has light-sensitive cells.
5. Which part of the brain is the seat of intelligence, memory and thinking?
   - Options: **cerebrum** / cerebellum / medulla oblongata / spinal cord
   - Explanation: The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain and controls voluntary actions and thought.

### Endocrine System

1. Insulin is secreted by the:
   - Options: **pancreas** / liver / thyroid / adrenal gland

2. A lack of iodine in the diet can cause:
   - Options: **goitre** / diabetes / dwarfism / scurvy

3. Which gland is called the master gland?
   - Options: **the pituitary gland** / the thyroid gland / the adrenal gland / the pancreas
   - Explanation: The pituitary controls several other endocrine glands.
4. Adrenaline is known as the hormone of:
   - Options: **fight or flight** / growth / sleep / digestion
   - Explanation: Adrenaline prepares the body for emergencies by raising heart rate and blood sugar.
5. A lack of insulin causes:
   - Options: **diabetes mellitus** / goitre / dwarfism / cretinism
